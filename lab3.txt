1. What is a benefit (besides aliasing) of our `SharedPtr<T>` directly storing a “stored pointer” (we could always just
   access it through the control block, if we're not aliasing)?
   Less transitions through the the control block, for comparisons and accesses we have less layers to travel through.
2. What is a disadvantage of having the stored pointer (instead of going through the control block)?
   More things to keep track of, and a higher chance of causing double frees or segmentation faults. (Ask me how I know)
3. From a system design/architecture perspective, what disadvantages are there to using shared pointers?
   It becomes hard to track ownership, ending up wondering who's keeping things in play. Could also end up in a situation where
   one object "owns" an object that "owns" itself, leading to an infinite lifespan.
4. From a performance perspective, what disadvantages there are to using shared pointers (say, versus a unique pointer
   or raw pointer)?
   We covered this one in class, but if the pointers are pointing to small objects, the size of the control block will
   stack up and use more memory than a non-shared pointer.
